# LinkedIn Launch Post — Hyrly (v1, 2026-05-21)

> **Goal:** announce Hyrly to your immediate LinkedIn network. Convert
> friends + ex-coworkers into early users + amplifiers. Reach the first
> non-friend laid-off engineer via reshares.
>
> **Audience:** your existing network (engineers, ex-coworkers, recruiters
> you've worked with). The post needs to convert your *network*; their
> shares get you to the actual ICP (laid-off engineers you don't know).
>
> **Stance:** founder-voice. Personal. Honest. Specific. Not "AI-powered
> career platform" marketing.

## Alternative hooks

Swap just the opening 2-3 lines if the primary doesn't feel right. Keep the body the same.

### Hook B — Contrarian thesis

```
Most laid-off engineers spend week 1 on the wrong things.

LinkedIn redesign. Resume perfectionism. Mass-applying to 200 jobs out of panic.

I watched it happen to ~30 friends over the last two years. Same pattern every time.
```

Trades the vulnerable opener for a thesis statement. Better for cold reach (people who don't know you), worse for warm network conversion (less personal).

### Hook C — Product-first

```
Today I'm launching Hyrly — the AI coach for engineers in their first 90 days post-layoff.

Built because nothing existed that helped my friends do the right things in week 1 after they got the news.

(Yes, "AI coach" is a tired phrase. Hear me out — this one's different on purpose.)
```

Honest about the "tired phrase" pre-empts the eye-roll. Better for a self-deprecating founder voice. Slightly weaker emotional hook than the primary.

## Execution plan

### Posting

- **Day/time:** Tuesday or Wednesday, 8-10am PT. Best LinkedIn slot for tech audience.
- **Image:** screenshot of the homepage hero (the "Just got laid off? / Don't update your resume yet." card). Higher CTR than no-image posts. Take it cleanly on desktop, crop to LinkedIn's 1200×627 ratio.
- **Hashtags:** keep light. `#layoffs #tech` at the end is enough. More than 3 looks spammy and the LinkedIn algorithm doesn't reward density.
- **Don't tag** anyone in the post itself unless they've pre-agreed. Tagging without permission burns goodwill and the algorithm penalizes posts where tagged people don't engage.

### First 60 minutes after posting

This window decides whether LinkedIn boosts the post or buries it.

- **Reply to every comment within 15 minutes.** Long, substantive replies. The algorithm reads reply length as engagement quality.
- **Pin a comment yourself** with a teaser of the severance calculator: *"For the cash-flow piece specifically — free severance calculator went live this week. It gives an honest dollar range based on your tier + tenure + leverage factors: https://hyrly.ai/tools/severance"*
- **Don't ask people to like/share in the post body** — algorithm penalty. Ask in the pinned reply if at all.

### Day-1 amplification

- DM your 10-15 closest engineering friends individually, BEFORE posting publicly. *"Hey — launching Hyrly today around 9am. The post will be on my profile; if you have a sec to leave a real comment in the first hour I'd appreciate it. (No need if it's not your thing — no pressure.)"* This gets you early engagement which the algorithm reads as quality signal.
- Cross-post to your X/Twitter the same day, but rewrite for X's format — shorter, threadable, less corporate.
- If you have a personal Substack or newsletter, send a launch email there. Same post, longer treatment, no algorithm dependency.

### Week-1 amplification

- Reshare your own post 48-72 hours later with one new angle: a specific user story, a new datapoint, or a follow-up on what people asked in the comments.
- DM 5 newsletter writers who cover layoffs/tech (Lenny's Newsletter, Pragmatic Engineer, layoffs.fyi). Specific ask: *"Built Hyrly for the laid-off engineer ICP. Won't pitch you to write about it, but if you'd link to the severance calculator in a future round-up, here's the URL."*
- Find 5 active Blind threads about recent layoffs. Don't shill — drop the severance calculator link in 1-2 of them where it's genuinely useful, then leave. (Blind users smell shilling fast and downvote heavily.)

### What success looks like

- **48 hours:** 20+ comments, 10+ reshares, 100+ profile visits, ~50 triage starts on hyrly.ai
- **7 days:** 1-2 newsletter mentions, first cold DM from someone you don't know saying it helped
- **30 days:** a recurring trickle of organic LinkedIn search traffic landing on /playbook articles via the launch post

### What failure looks like

- 5-10 comments, all from people you know personally, no reshares. → The hook didn't land. Try Hook B next week with a real user story as the lead.

## Things to NOT do

- **Don't** post a "Big news! 🚀🎉 I'm so excited to announce..." version. The audience is laid-off engineers; the tone needs to match the moment.
- **Don't** mention pricing in the launch post. Triage + Scout are free for the first 3 sessions; talking about $29/mo upfront makes it feel like a SaaS pitch.
- **Don't** name specific competitors (LinkedIn, Indeed, etc.) negatively in the post body. Implied contrast is fine; explicit attacks make enemies and the algorithm flags it as negative content.
- **Don't** delete and re-post if it doesn't perform. LinkedIn's algorithm pattern-matches that as inauthentic. Let it ride and reshare with a new angle in 48 hours.

## Open editorial decisions for you

1. **"Three of my closest friends" → exact number?** Three is intimate and specific. If you'd rather not name a count, use *"close friends"* (vague but warm). If the real number is higher, *"more than I want to admit"* is also good.
2. **Country specificity?** Currently says "US tech engineers." If you want to widen to international, drop the "US." If you want to narrow further (e.g. "Bay Area engineers"), add it — narrower wedges convert better in this kind of post.
3. **Hashtags vs no hashtags?** Both work. Skip them if the audience is exclusively your network; include them if you want any cold-search reach.
4. **Mention the H-1B angle in the post body?** The current draft does — *"60-day visa clock (if you're on H-1B)"*. If your network is largely non-H-1B you could cut it. But this is one of Hyrly's actual differentiators and it's mentioned in passing, so I'd leave it.
