## B. Standalone follow-up post (use 2-3 days after the launch)

Best slot: Tuesday or Wednesday, ~9am PT. Different hook from the launch — leads with the 60-70% asymmetry stat, not the personal story.

```
60-70% of laid-off engineers don't counter their severance offer.

Companies expect counter-offers. HR usually has authority to improve packages within a defined band. But 2 out of 3 of you sign the first paper.

→ Don't sign for 48-72 hours. The first number is rarely the final number.

→ Leverage that genuinely shifts the number: tenure 4+ years, age 40+ (ADEA protection), recent FMLA or pregnancy, WARN Act exposure (50+ at one site).

No signup. Same numbers the Hyrly Playbook severance article uses.

If this helps someone you know, share it.
```

## Execution notes

### For format A (pinned comment)
- Post the launch first. Wait 5-10 minutes. Then drop the pinned comment yourself.
- This is one of the highest-conversion patterns on LinkedIn — the original post hooks attention, the pinned comment converts the most-interested 20% to the actual tool.
- Don't post the comment instantly — algorithm reads pre-meditated comments as lower quality.

### For format B (standalone follow-up)
- Wait at least 48 hours after the launch. Different week is even better — Tuesday after the Sunday launch, or the following Tuesday.
- Don't reshare or quote your launch from this post. Let it stand alone.
- If the launch had a great comment thread with someone sharing their own negotiation win, lead with that quote instead of the 60-70% stat (real-user social proof always beats a stat).

### Anti-patterns
- **Don't** post both the pinned comment AND the standalone the same day. Same calculator + two surfaces = looks promotional.
- **Don't** edit the launch post to add the calculator link in the main body retroactively. LinkedIn penalizes edited posts, and your existing engagement won't transfer cleanly.
- **Don't** use this calculator post as a quote-tweet of someone else's layoff thread. The 60-70% line + the dollar specifics are too direct for that context; would feel like ambulance-chasing.
